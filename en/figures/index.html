The mental load in couples: the 2026 figures

Figures as of 26 November 2026 · Who thinks of it? study (Qui y pense ?, France)

Since September 2026, 27,819 woman-man couples have taken stock of 50 household tasks, with two questions per task: who does it, and who thinks of it. Here is what their answers show, with the method and its limits. Most respondents used the French site.

84 %of couples where she carries more than half of the mental load
64 %of the mental load carried by her, on average

The essentials in six sentences

  • In 84% of woman-man couples, she carries more than half of the household's mental load (27,819 couples, Who thinks of it? study, November 2026).
  • On average, she carries 64% of the household's mental load, meaning the things that have to be kept in mind.
  • The figure depends on who answers: 69% when she fills it in alone, 48% when he fills it in alone, 61% when they fill it in together.
  • Her share rises with the number of children: 60% with no children, 66% with three or more.
  • When both partners work full time, she spends 31 hours a week on the home and he spends 21 (11,950 couples).
  • With paid work included, total time is shared almost equally (a median of 53% for her), but the mental load does not follow: 66% for her when both work full time with two children.

The mental load is measured by the question "who thinks of it?", asked for every task: thinking of it means noticing, planning and checking, and also having to ask or remind. The table gives the average share carried by her.

The questionnaire asks who is filling it in. On the same questions, the answer changes markedly depending on whether it is her, him, or both together.

These are not two partners in the same couple contradicting each other: they are different couples. Part of the gap comes from who visits the site, and part from the fact that each of us sees first what we do ourselves. More in the article She fills it in, he fills it in.

The method and its limits

  • Measured task by task. This is not a poll asking "who does more?". Each couple goes through 50 tasks, from the most common to the rarest.
  • A mental load weighted by frequency. A task thought of every week or more weighs three times, every month twice, less often once.
  • Volunteer couples, not a representative sample. They are visitors to the site, who came mainly through LinkedIn, word of mouth and an article in Marie Claire (France). More than two thirds of the answers are filled in by her, which is why the figures are also given by who fills it in.
  • The couples included. 30,007 answers received as of 26 November 2026, of which 27,819 woman-man couples who answered at least 20 tasks.
  • Anonymous answers. No first names, no address, no email, no IP address. Only aggregated results are published.
  • The full calculation is on the Method page (in French).

Frequently asked questions

What percentage of the mental load do women carry?

In the Who thinks of it? study, she carries 64% of the household's mental load on average, and more than half in 84% of woman-man couples (27,819 couples, November 2026).

Are these figures representative?

No. They come from volunteer couples who took stock on the site, most of them in France. They describe these 27,819 couples, not the whole population. The gaps between sub-groups have been stable since the first 400 answers.

How is the mental load measured?

For each of the 50 tasks, the couple says who thinks of it. Each person's share is weighted by how often the task comes round. Thinking is not converted into minutes.

When will the figures be updated?

The page is updated as the study goes on, with the date at the top. The full barometer will be published on 8 March 2027.
