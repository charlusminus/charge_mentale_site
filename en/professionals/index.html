Who thinks of it? › Professionals

A session aid, free and with no sign-up

Couple counselling, family therapy, family mediation, parenting workshops: Who thinks of it? gives a couple something concrete to talk about who does what at home, and above all who thinks of it. 50 tasks, about ten minutes, a result to read together.

What the couple gets

  • For each of the 50 tasks, who does it, measured in hours a day, and who thinks of it, measured in things to keep in mind: the mental load, told apart from the time spent.
  • A result that does not judge: it does not blame anyone and does not compare with a norm. Ideas for rebalancing only appear when the time leans clearly one way, and never towards the person already carrying more.
  • Two ways to answer: together, or each on their own, then both views side by side.

Three ways to use it

  1. Between sessions. Suggest the couple take stock at home, each on their own, and bring the link to their result. The link holds the answers: opened in the session, it shows the same result, with no sign-up and no password.
  2. In a session. The couple fills it in together, on a phone or a computer. The gaps between what each of them believed and what comes up ("I didn't know you were thinking of that") are often the best starting point.
  3. In a group workshop (parenting groups, community centres, workplaces). Each couple, or each participant, takes stock on their phone, then the group talks it through. The outline below fits on one page.

A ninety-minute workshop

10 minWelcome. The mental load in one sentence: doing a task and thinking of it are two different jobs (noticing it needs doing, planning, reminding, checking). More on the mental load.
15 minEveryone takes stock on quiypense.org/en, alone or as a couple.
30 minSmall groups, three questions. Which task surprised you? What do you carry without the other person seeing it? Which task would you like to hand over completely, including thinking of it?
25 minBack in the whole group. What comes up from one couple to the next, and what people have already tried.
10 minClose. One task to take back or hand over before next time, and the idea of taking stock again in six months.

Nobody has to show their result to the group: everyone keeps their screen to themselves and shares what they want. This page prints as it is.

What to know before suggesting it

It is

  • A conversation aid, based on the French national time-use surveys (INSEE)
  • Free, with no sign-up
  • Independent: no partner has any presence in the tool

It is not

  • A diagnosis, or a clinically validated test
  • A follow-up tool: the site keeps neither first names nor result links
  • Hidden data collection: an anonymised copy goes to a study on how housework is shared, and this is announced before the result (privacy). Tell participants.
