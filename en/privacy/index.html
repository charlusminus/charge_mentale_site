Privacy

Who thinks of it? helps two people take stock together, with no sign-up. Here, in plain words, is what is collected, why, by whom, for how long, and how to exercise your rights.

Who is responsible

Qui y pense ? (quiypense.org), whose English version is called Who thinks of it? Contact for any question or request: contact@quiypense.org.

What stays with you

The calculation happens in your browser. Your answers and your first names are encoded in the page address (the sharing link), and the Excel file is made on your own computer. The site keeps neither this link nor your first names.

What is collected

WhatWhy, and on what basisHow long
The anonymised copy for the study: your answers, the durations you set, the number of children, the gender given, who filled it in, any tasks you added, your paid working hours if you give them, your age bracket, occupation and county (French department) if you give them, the version of the questionnaire, the source of the visit and a scrambled fingerprint of the household number. Never a first name, an email address or an IP address. To carry out the study on how tasks are shared and to publish benchmarks, always aggregated. Your consent, asked for before the result (without it, the result is not shown). For as long as the study lasts. If you correct your result, the copy keeps being updated for 24 hours.
Your feedback, if you give it: a rating, “did it help you?”, a free comment (email addresses and phone numbers removed automatically). To improve the site. Your consent, by sending it.For as long as the study lasts.
Your email address, only if you leave it in the “online save” form, along with your answer about the price. To write to you once, if online saving comes about. Your consent, by sending it. Until that message, and two years at most. Deleted sooner if you simply ask.
Audience measurement (Google Analytics): pages viewed, where visitors come from, device, a few clicks, using cookies. Never your answers. To know what is useful on the site. Only with your consent, given in the banner and changeable at any time through the “Cookies” link at the bottom of the page. Google Analytics cookies: 13 months at most. Your choice is kept in your browser.

To check that something sent to the site does not come from a robot (copy for the study, feedback, email address), the site uses Cloudflare Turnstile, which analyses the request, including the IP address, for the time this check takes. The technical services that receive something sent to the site also see the IP address for the time of the request; it is never recorded with your answers.

From an assistant (Claude, ChatGPT…)

If you take stock from an assistant, through the Who thinks of it? MCP server, the conversation is processed by the company that provides the assistant, under its own policy. The server only receives the answers to the questionnaire, never your first names, and only sends the anonymised copy for the study with your consent, just as on the site. It keeps nothing from one request to the next.

Who else is involved

No data is sold or handed to a partner, a brand or an employer (see the charter, in French). Only technical providers are involved, on behalf of the site:

  • GitHub (hosting the site);
  • Google (Google Sheets and Apps Script for the study and feedback, Google Analytics for audience measurement);
  • Cloudflare (Turnstile, and hosting the MCP server);
  • n8n (the “online save” form).

Some of them are based in the United States; these transfers rely on their contractual commitments (the European Commission's standard contractual clauses or the EU-US Data Privacy Framework). The “Support” button leads to Ko-fi, a third-party site with its own policy: nothing from your result is passed on to it.

Your rights

You can ask to access, correct or delete your data, object to its processing or withdraw your consent, by writing to contact@quiypense.org. For the copy for the study, which contains nothing that identifies you, include the link to your result: its household number lets us find and delete your copies. You can also contact the CNIL, the French data protection authority (cnil.fr). If you live in the UK, you can also contact the ICO (Information Commissioner's Office, ico.org.uk).
